Books

Books I keep going back to, with a note on each.

  • Atomic Habits

    James Clear, 2018

    James Clear explains habit change through systems: make useful behaviors obvious and easy, then make unwanted ones difficult. The advice is practical and repetitive by design, with enough examples to turn an idea into a routine.

    5 passages worth keeping
    • You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems.
    • Every action you take is a vote for the type of person you wish to become.
    • Habits are the compound interest of self-improvement.
    • The most effective form of learning is practice, not planning.
    • Success is the product of daily habits, not once-in-a-lifetime transformations.

    On Goodreads

  • Deep Work

    Cal Newport, 2016

    Cal Newport makes the case for long, distraction-free stretches of difficult work. The useful parts are concrete: schedule focus, protect it with rituals, and cut the shallow tasks that keep filling the day.

    5 passages worth keeping
    • Deep work is the ability to focus without distraction on a cognitively demanding task.
    • The ability to perform deep work is becoming increasingly rare at the same time it is becoming increasingly valuable.
    • To produce at your peak level you need to work for extended periods with full concentration on a single task free from distraction.
    • Clarity about what matters provides clarity about what does not.
    • The best moments usually occur when a person’s body or mind is stretched to its limits in a voluntary effort to accomplish something difficult and worthwhile.

    On Goodreads

  • Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind

    Yuval Noah Harari, 2014

    Yuval Noah Harari tells human history through our ability to cooperate around shared stories about money, religion, and nations. It is sweeping and provocative, and it reads best as a set of arguments to test rather than a settled account.

    5 passages worth keeping
    • The real difference between us and chimpanzees is the mysterious glue that enables millions of humans to cooperate effectively.
    • Money is the most universal and most efficient system of mutual trust ever devised.
    • The most important thing to know about prehistoric humans is that they were insignificant animals with no more impact on their environment than gorillas, fireflies or jellyfish.
    • Culture tends to argue that it forbids only that which is unnatural. But from a biological perspective, nothing is unnatural.
    • History is something that very few people have been doing while everyone else was ploughing fields and carrying water buckets.

    On Goodreads

  • Thinking, Fast and Slow

    Daniel Kahneman, 2011

    Daniel Kahneman explains how fast intuition and slower deliberation shape judgment. The experiments are the heart of the book: anchoring, loss aversion, and overconfidence become easier to spot once you have seen how they work.

    5 passages worth keeping
    • The confidence that individuals have in their beliefs depends mostly on the quality of the story they can tell about what they see.
    • The easiest way to increase happiness is to control your use of time.
    • We can be blind to the obvious, and we are also blind to our blindness.
    • A reliable way to make people believe in falsehoods is frequent repetition.
    • The world makes much less sense than you think. The coherence comes mostly from the way your mind works.

    On Goodreads

  • The Art of War

    Sun Tzu, 5th Century BC

    Thirteen short chapters examine preparation, terrain, deception, and the cost of conflict. The writing is terse enough to travel well beyond its military setting, though its business-book afterlife often smooths away the harsher parts.

    5 passages worth keeping
    • The supreme art of war is to subdue the enemy without fighting.
    • If you know the enemy and know yourself, you need not fear the result of a hundred battles.
    • Appear weak when you are strong, and strong when you are weak.
    • The greatest victory is that which requires no battle.
    • Supreme excellence consists of breaking the enemy’s resistance without fighting.

    On Goodreads

  • Meditations

    Marcus Aurelius, 180 AD

    These are private notes from a Roman emperor reminding himself how to behave under pressure. The repetition feels honest: patience, duty, mortality, and control were problems Marcus Aurelius had to work through more than once.

    5 passages worth keeping
    • The happiness of your life depends upon the quality of your thoughts.
    • Waste no more time arguing about what a good man should be. Be one.
    • Accept the things to which fate binds you, and love the people with whom fate brings you together.
    • If it is not right do not do it; if it is not true do not say it.
    • The best revenge is not to be like your enemy.

    On Goodreads
